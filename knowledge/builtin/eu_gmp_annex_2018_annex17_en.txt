Annex 17: Real Time Release Testing and Parametric Release
Legal basis for publishing the detailed guidelines: Article 47 of Directive 2001/83/EC on the
Community code relating to medicinal products for human use and Article 51 of Directive
2001/82/EC on the Community code relating to veterinary medicinal products. This document
provides guidance for the interpretation of the principles and guidelines of good manufacturing
practice (GMP) for medicinal products as laid down in Directive 2003/94/EC for medicinal
products for human use and Directive 91/412/EEC for veterinary use.
Status of the document: Revision 1
Reasons for changes: The previous guideline only focused on the application of Parametric
Release for the routine release of terminally sterilised products waiving the performance of a test
for sterility on the basis of successful demonstration that predetermined and validated sterilising
conditions have been achieved. Moreover, advances in the application of process analytical
technology (PAT), quality by design (QbD) and quality risk management (QRM) principles to
pharmaceutical development and manufacturing have shown that an appropriate combination of
process controls together with timely monitoring and verification of pre-established material
attributes provides greater assurance of product quality than finished product testing
(conventionally regarded as the end-product testing) alone.
This revision to Annex 17 takes into account changes to other sections of the EudraLex, Volume 4,
Part I, Chapter 1, Annex 1 and 15, ICH Q8, Q9, Q10 and Q11, QWP Guideline on Real Time
Release Testing, and changes in manufacturing and analytical technology.
Deadline for coming into operation: 26 December 2018 (6 months after publication]

1. Principle	1
2
1.1 Medicinal products must comply with their approved specifications and subject to compliance	3
with GMP, can normally be released to market by performing a complete set of tests on active	4
substances and/or finished products as defined in the relevant marketing authorization or clinical	5
trial authorization. In specific circumstances, where authorised, based on product knowledge and	6
process understanding, information collected during the manufacturing process can be used instead	7
of end-product testing for batch release. Any separate activities required for this form of batch	8
release should be integrated into the Pharmaceutical Quality System (PQS).	9
10
2. Scope	11
12
2.1 This document is intended to outline the requirements for application of Real Time Release	13
Testing (RTRT) and parametric release, where the control of critical parameters and relevant	14
material attributes are authorized as an alternative to routine end-product testing of active	15
substances and/or finished products. A specific aim of this guideline is to incorporate the	16
application of RTRT to any stage in the manufacturing process and to any type of finished products	17
or active substances, including their intermediates.	18

3. Real time release testing (RTRT)	20
21
3.1 Under RTRT, a combination of in-process monitoring and controls may provide, when	22
authorized, substitute for end-product testing as part of the batch release decision.	23
Interaction with all relevant regulatory authorities prior and during the assessment process	24
preceding regulatory approval is required. The level of interaction will depend on the level of	25
complexity of the RTRT control procedure applied on site.	26
27
3.2 When designing the RTRT strategy, the following minimum criteria are expected to be	28
established and met:	29
30
(i) Real time measurement and control of relevant in-process material attributes and process	31
parameters should be accurate predictors of the corresponding finished product attributes.	32
(ii) The valid combination of relevant assessed material attributes and process controls to	33
replace finished product attributes should be established with scientific evidence based on	34
material, product and process knowledge.	35
(iii) The combined process measurements (process parameters and material attributes) and any	36
other test data generated during the manufacturing process should provide a robust	37
foundation for RTRT and the batch release decision.	38
39
3.3 A RTRT strategy should be integrated and controlled through the PQS. This should include or	40
reference information at least of the following:	41
- quality risk management, including a full process related risk assessment, in accordance with	42
the principles described in EudraLex, Volume 4, Part I Chapter 1 and Part II Chapter 2,	43
- change control program,	44
- control strategy,	45
- specific personnel training program,	46
- qualification and validation policy,	47

- deviation/CAPA system,	48
- contingency procedure in case of a process sensor/equipment failure,	49
- periodic review/assessment program to measure the effectiveness of the RTRT plan for	50
continued assurance of product quality.	51

3.4 In accordance with the principles described in EudraLex, Volume 4, Part I Chapter 1, Part II	53
Chapter 13 and Annex 15, the change control program is an important part of the real time release	54
testing approach. Any change that could potentially impact product manufacturing and testing, or	55
the validated status of facilities, systems, equipment, analytical methods or processes, should be	56
assessed for risk to product quality and impact on reproducibility of the manufacturing process. Any	57
change should be justified by the sound application of quality risk management principles, and fully	58
documented. After change implementation, an evaluation should be undertaken to demonstrate that	59
there are no unintended or deleterious impact on product quality.	60
61
3.5 A control strategy should be designed not only to monitor the process, but also to maintain a	62
state of control and ensure that a product of the required quality will be consistently produced. The	63
control strategy should describe and justify the selected in-process controls, material attributes and	64
process parameters which require to be routinely monitored and should be based on product,	65
formulation and process understanding. The control strategy is dynamic and may change throughout	66
the lifecycle of the product requiring the use of a quality risk management approach and of	67
knowledge management. The control strategy should also describe the sampling plan and	68
acceptance/rejection criteria.	69
70
3.6 Personnel should be given specific training on RTRT technologies, principles and procedures.	71
Key personnel should demonstrate adequate experience, product and process knowledge and	72
understanding. Successful implementation of RTRT requires input from a cross-functional/multi-	73
disciplinary team with relevant experience on specific topics, such as engineering, analytics,	74
chemometric modeling or statistics.	75
76
3.7 Important parts of the RTRT strategy are validation and qualification policy, with particular	77
reference to advanced analytical methods. Particular attention should be focused on the	78
qualification, validation and management of in-line and on-line analytical methods, where the	79
sampling probe is placed within the manufacturing equipment.	80
81
3.8 Any deviation or process failure should be thoroughly investigated and any adverse trending	82
indicating a change in the state of control should be followed up appropriately.	83
84
3.9 Continuous learning through data collection and analysis over the life cycle of a product is	85
important and should be part of the PQS. With advances in technology, certain data trends, intrinsic	86
to a currently acceptable process, may be observed. Manufacturers should scientifically evaluate the	87
data, in consultation if appropriate, with the regulatory authorities, to determine how or if such	88
trends indicate opportunities to improve quality and/or consistency.	89
90

4. Parametric release and sterilization	103
104
4.1 This section provides guidance on parametric release which is defined as the release of a batch	105
of terminally sterilised product based on a review of critical process control parameters rather than	106
requiring an end-product testing for sterility.	107
108
4.2 An end-product test for sterility is limited in its ability to detect contamination as it utilises only	109
a small number of samples in relation to the overall batch size, and secondly, culture media may	110
only stimulate growth of some, but not all, microorganisms. Therefore, an end-product testing for	111
sterility only provides an opportunity to detect major failures in the sterility assurance system (i.e. a	112
failure that results in contamination of a large number of product units and/or that result in	113
contamination by the specific microorganisms whose growth is supported by the prescribed media).	114
In contrast, data derived from in-process controls (e.g. pre-sterilization product bioburden or	115
environmental monitoring) and by monitoring relevant sterilization parameters can provide more	116
accurate and relevant information to support sterility assurance of the product.	117
118
4.3 Parametric release can only be applied to products sterilised in their final container using either	119
moist heat, dry heat or ionising radiation (dosimetric release), according to European	120
Pharmacopoeial requirements.	121
122
4.4 To utilise this approach, the manufacturer should have a history of acceptable GMP compliance	123
and a robust sterility assurance program in place to demonstrate consistent process control and	124
process understanding.	125
126
4.5 The sterility assurance program should be documented and include, at least, the identification	127
and monitoring of the critical process parameters, sterilizer cycle development and validation,	128
container/packaging integrity validation, bioburden control, environmental monitoring program,	129
product segregation plan, equipment, services and facility design and qualification program,	130
maintenance and calibration program, change control program, personnel training, and incorporate	131
a quality risk management approach.	132
133
4.6 Risk management is an essential requirement for parametric release and should focus on	134
mitigating the factors which increase the risk of failure to achieve and maintain sterility in each unit	135
of every batch. If a new product or process is being considered for parametric release, then a risk	136
assessment should be conducted during process development including an evaluation of production	137
data from existing products if applicable. If an existing product or process is being considered, the	138
risk assessment should include an evaluation of any historical data generated.	139

4.7 Personnel involved in the parametric release process should have experience in the following	141
areas: microbiology, sterility assurance, engineering, production and sterilization. The	142
qualifications, experience, competency and training of all personnel involved in parametric release	143
should be documented.	144
145
4.8 Any proposed change which may impact on sterility assurance should be recorded in the change	146
control system and reviewed by appropriate personnel who are qualified and experienced in sterility	147
assurance.	148
149
4.9 A pre-sterilization bio-burden monitoring program for the product and components should be	150
developed to support parametric release. The bioburden should be performed for each batch. The	151
sampling locations of filled units before sterilization should be based on a worst-case scenario and	152
be representative of the batch. Any organisms found during bioburden testing should be identified	153
to confirm that they are not spore forming which may be more resistant to the sterilizing process.	154
155
4.10 Product bio-burden should be minimized by appropriate design of the manufacturing	156
environment and the process by:	157
- good equipment and facility design to allow effective cleaning, disinfection and sanitisation;	158
- availability of detailed and effective procedures for cleaning, disinfection and sanitisation;	159
- use of microbial retentive filters where possible;	160
- availability of operating practices and procedures which promote personnel hygiene and enforce	161
appropriate garment control;	162
- - appropriate microbiological specifications for raw materials, intermediates and process aids	163
(e.g. gases)	164
165
4.11 For aqueous or otherwise microbiologically unstable products, the time lag between dissolving	166
the starting materials, product fluid filtration, and sterilization should be defined in order to	167
minimise the development of bioburden and an increase in endotoxins (if applicable).	168

Sterilization Process	170
171
4.12 Qualification and validation are critical activities to assure that sterilization equipment can	172
consistently meet cycle operational parameters and that the monitoring devices provide verification	173
of the sterilization process.	174
175
4.13 Periodic requalification of equipment and revalidation of processes should be planned and	176
justified in accordance with the requirements of Annexes 1 and 15.	177
178
4.14 Appropriate measurement of critical process parameters during sterilization is a critical	179
requirement in a parametric release program. The standards used for process measuring devices	180
should be specified and the calibration should be traceable to national or international standards.	181
182
4.15 Critical process parameters should be established, defined and undergo periodic re-evaluation.	183
The operating ranges should be developed based on sterilization process, process capability,	184
calibration tolerance limits and parameter criticality.	185

4.16 Routine monitoring of the sterilizer should demonstrate that the validated conditions necessary	187
to achieve the specified process is achieved in each cycle. Critical processes should be specifically	188
monitored during the sterilization phase.	189
190
4.17 The sterilization record should include all the critical process parameters. The sterilization	191
records should be checked for compliance to specification by at least two independent systems.	192
These systems may consist of two people or a validated computer system plus a person.	193
194
4.18 Once parametric release has been approved by the regulatory authorities, decisions for release	195
or rejection of a batch should be based on the approved specifications and the review of critical	196
process control data. Routine checks of the sterilizer, changes, deviations, unplanned and routine	197
planned maintenance activities should be recorded, assessed and approved before releasing the	198
products to the market. Non-compliance with the specification for parametric release cannot be	199
overruled by a finished product passing the test for sterility.	200

5. Glossary	202
203
Control strategy	204
A planned set of controls, derived from current product and process understanding that ensures	205
process performance and product quality. The controls can include parameters and attributes related	206
to drug substance and drug product materials and components, facility and equipment operating	207
conditions, in-process controls, finished product specifications, and the associated methods and	208
frequency of monitoring and control.	209
210
Critical Process Parameters:	211
A process parameter whose variability has an impact on a critical quality attribute and therefore	212
should be monitored or controlled to ensure the process produces the desired quality [ICH Q8	213
(R2)].	214
215
Critical Quality Attributes	216
A physical, chemical, biological, or microbiological property or characteristic that should be within	217
an appropriate limit, range, or distribution to ensure the desired product quality. [ICH Q8 (R2)]	218
219
Parametric release	220
One form of RTRT. Parametric release for terminally sterilised product is based on the review of	221
documentation on process monitoring (e.g. temperature, pressure, time for terminal sterilization)	222
rather than the testing of a sample for a specific attribute (ICH Q8 Q&A). (Together with	223
compliance with specific GMP requirements related to parametric release this provides the desired	224
assurance of the quality of the product.) (EMA guideline on Real-Time Release Testing)	225
226
Real time release testing	227
The ability to evaluate and ensure the quality of in-process and/or final product based on process	228
data, which typically include a valid combination of measured material attributes and process	229
controls. (ICH Q8)	230
231
State of Control	232
A condition in which the set of controls consistently provides assurance of continued process	233
performance and product quality. (ICH Q10)	234
